# Experimental noise literature database

This is a literature record for a future superconducting surface-code simulation. It is not a simulation design. Values are copied as reported, with the evidence type preserved. A dash means the cited experiment did not report that field in the material reviewed. Values from different devices are never combined into one device model.

## Scope and evidence labels

- **Directly measured:** reported as an experimental measurement on the named device.
- **Fitted:** obtained by fitting an experimental decay, spectrum, or distribution.
- **Derived from same-device measurements:** calculated only from compatible values in the same experiment. No QEC-round persistence is derived from `T1`, `T2`, or a gate error unless the source explicitly reports it.
- **Theoretical/model parameter:** used in the paper's model or protocol analysis, not independently measured as the quantity itself.
- **Not reported:** no defensible value was located in the cited source material.

## Important interpretation constraints

1. `T1` and `T2` describe decay of a qubit state or coherence under a specified experiment. They are not automatically the temporal correlation time of the noise process.
2. A leakage lifetime is the persistence of population outside the computational subspace. In the Google leakage-injection experiment it is approximately 4.4 QEC cycles, which is directly compatible with the reported approximately 1 microsecond cycle. That value should not be transferred to another processor or gate schedule.
3. A 1/f spectrum does not define a unique finite correlation time without explicit low- and high-frequency cutoffs. The PSD exponent and cutoff parameters must be retained.
4. A calibration cadence, such as 10 ms or 107 ms, is the time required to measure or update a parameter. It is not by itself a physical noise-memory time.
5. Rare-event rates such as one event per hour do not specify the duration of an individual burst. Burst duration must be measured separately.
6. Surface-code detector-pair probabilities are collective circuit-level observations. They should not be converted into independent per-gate probabilities without a stated model.
